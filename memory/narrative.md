# Narrative Brief
**Owner: Miles.** Rules for every generated region, boss, weapon, card and dialogue line in Doom Scroller.

## Tone
Sincere fantasy adventure in the spirit of New World, Zelda, WoW and The Lord of the Rings. Monsters are dangerous, places are worth seeing, treasure is worth finding.
- **It is:** earnest, adventurous, classic high fantasy.
- **It is never:** campy, memey, grimdark or melancholy.

| Rule | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Describe things plainly and sincerely. | "A lean grey wolf, hungry and hunting alone." | "Ye olde wolfe, most fearsome of beasties!" |
| No modern slang, emoji or jokes at the world's expense. | "A scaled beast that sleeps in warm cinders and wakes hungry." | "This skeleton is lowkey built different." |
| Danger without gore or despair. | "A boar bristling with quills, already charging." | "Its jaws drip with the blood of children." |
| Card text never says "you", except in satire beats. | "A slime that oozes along the roadside." | "You see a slime. It looks at you." |

**Satire:** slight, witty references that break the fourth wall a little by nodding at the player's habits. The same in every region. At most 1 in 15 cards and 1 in 15 dialogue lines; never more than one satirical line per village visit; none in the story opening.
- Good: "Gold rings and a ruby, still here because most travellers never stop to look."
- Bad: "Don't just scroll past, smash that like on this goblin!" (too loud, banned words)

## Premise
- **The hero:** one of many adventurers. Appearance and build are customised by the player. No name, personality, backstory, feelings or dialogue.
- **Why they travel:** they took up adventuring to get stronger.
- **The world:** full of monsters that have existed since the world of old. Most are natural, akin to animals; undead come from necromancers and the ancient dungeons. Bandits and evil humans also exist. Adventurers make it safe to inhabit by fighting monsters and taking quests from village quest boards.
- **Stakes:** whether the world becomes safe to inhabit. The greatest threat is the final raid boss in the Volcano.

## World
Region names are working names. Each solo boss drops the key item that opens the next region.

| # | Region | Look | Lives there | Solo boss | Key item |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Green Valley | Rolling grassland, farms | Animals, slimes, goblins | A massive roc | Roc Pinion |
| 2 | Forest | Dense old woodland | Tree people, golems, large predators, skeletons | A stone golem with trees growing from its back | Golem Core |
| 3 | Desert | Dunes, sun-bleached stone | Small sphinxes, imps, devils | A sphinx | Sphinx's Guile |
| 4 | Ocean | Open sea, beaches, villages on turtles' backs | Merfolk, sea monsters, flying water wyrms, water elementals | A kraken | Kraken's Eye |
| 5 | Swamp | Tar pits and bogs holding dead giants | Skeletons, giant skeletons, zombies, dark wizards, dark tree creatures, mud golems | A reforming skeleton amalgamation; beat its necromancer (a separate card) first | Giant Marrow |
| 6 | Mountain | Snow, rock, troll encampments | Dire wolves, militaristic trolls, small ice wyrms, ice golems, cloud monsters, typhons | The Troll General | Troll's Burning Heart |
| 7 | Volcano | Lava and ash | Dragons, wyrms, fire elementals, charred skeletons | An ancient dragon | (final region) |

- **Sky City:** a floating city and its own region, reached with the Sphinx's Guile; reaching it unlocks the Ocean. A village players return to. Unnamed: call it "the Sky City". Never invent its features.
- **Raid boss:** a magma elemental at the heart of the Volcano. Drops Volcanic Ore, used in strong crafting recipes.
- **Villages:** many generic villages, art themed to their region. Each has a Tavern, Inn, Market, Blacksmith, Alchemist and quest board.
- **Dungeons:** ancient dungeons, built by the ancient race (unnamed: call them "the ancient race"), are scattered across all regions, which ties certain monsters to certain areas. In lore they can turn people into zombies or skeletons, with no gameplay effect. Monster lairs are natural places, not constructed.
- **Existing content:** all names are provisional. Ancient: Bonewrought Depths, Emberforge Ruins, Hollow Crown Crypt, Sunken Cistern. Lairs: Widow's Thicket, Frostfang Hollow (lair of the Frostfang Direwolf). Renamed: Frost-Touched Wraith → Frostbound Wraith; Frostfang → Frostfang Direwolf; Wolf → Grey Wolf; Frozen Cache → Frozen Ice Shelf; Wooden Sword → Hallow Wood Sword. Bandit, Highway Cutthroat and Ironclad Raider are human.

## Story opening
- **Setting:** a village in the Green Valley.
- **Delivered by:** the Innkeeper, speaking to the new adventurer, plus info cards.
- **Tone:** no satire; the fourth wall is broken only to teach screens and mechanics.
- **Beats:** (1) The Innkeeper welcomes the adventurer and explains the world. (2) How to move around the village and what each place does. (3) The quest board. (4) Outside the gates: equip gear, then fight a goblin that got too close. (5) Back to the village.
- **Intro lines are not written yet. Do not generate intro or tutorial text until the core mechanics are final.**
- **Info cards:** intro and tutorial only; same length limits as other cards; the only cards exempt from the banned-word list.

## Naming rules
- **General:** plain English mixed with made-up words. Made-up words come only from this approved list: Skarn, Vharok, Dravik, Torvun, Grisk, Ulmar. Apostrophes allowed; hyphens never. No profanity or sexual content. Card titles ≤ 35 characters.
- **Enemies:** a monster type (approved word or English creature) plus English or fantasy adjectives, up to three words. Good: "Quillback Boar", "Ashen Skarn". Bad: "Frost-Touched Wraith" (hyphen), "Elder Frozen Bone Wraith" (four words).
- **Bosses:** a personal name from the approved list plus a short title. Good: "Vharok the Troll General". Bad: "Vharok, Unbowed General of the High Peaks" (too long).
- **Weapons:** rarity word first, then a distinct name, never a generic one. Rarity, lowest to highest: Plain, Tempered, Runed, Relic, Exalted. Good: "Tempered Hallow Wood Sword". Bad: "Tempered Sword".
- **Abilities:** distinct, short names. Good: "Rend", "Embolden". Bad: "Super Slash Attack".
- **Keepers:** Innkeeper, Tavernkeep, Shopkeep, Blacksmith, Alchemist, Wandering Merchant. Exception: Sky City keepers get unique personal names from the approved list.
- **Places:** a compound or descriptive name plus a place type. Good: "Emberforge Ruins", "Widow's Thicket". Bad: "The Ruins".
- **Resource nodes:** plain names. Good: "Iron Vein", "Large Boulder".

## Voices
All dialogue is spoken at the player, who never responds: open-ended questions or statements, up to two short sentences. Good: "Coming in to buy something?" Bad: "What's your name, traveller?" (asks for a reply). A warbling pixel-talk sound plays as each dialogue box opens.

| Keeper | Personality and speech | Sample line |
|---|---|---|
| Innkeeper | Lighter voice, warm and welcoming. | "Rest up. The road will still be there in the morning." |
| Tavernkeep | Lighter voice, excited and fast; short bursts. | "Sit, sit! What'll it be? Got stew, got stories!" |
| Shopkeep | Friendly haggler who loves a deal. | "For you, a special price. Well, nearly special!" |
| Blacksmith | Larger person at the forge, deeper voice; short, heavy sentences. | "Bring me iron. I'll make it worth carrying." |
| Alchemist | Slow and methodical; measured, precise wording. | "Three drops. No more. Patience makes the potion." |
| Wandering Merchant | One personality per appearance: shifty, cheerful, mysterious, pushy or weary. | "Coming in to buy something?" |

- **Bosses:** some speak before a fight, mid-fight and on defeat, in statements. Sample: "I'll take you down."
- **Quest board:** non-diegetic, written for the player, with a clear goal and counter. Good: "Kill 10 goblins 0/10".

## Sample card text
- **Enemy**: Grisk Goblin: "A wiry goblin clutching a stolen kitchen knife." / Bog Dravik: "A long-limbed swamp hunter that waits beneath the tar."
- **Ambush**: Goblin Raiding Party: "Spears rattle in the tall grass as goblins rush the road." / Merfolk Raiders: "Merfolk burst from the surf with tridents raised."
- **Treasure** (describes the loot): Buried Coin Purse: "A rotted leather purse spilling old silver coins." / Sunken Sea Chest: "Barnacled gold coins and a pearl the size of an eye."
- **Resource** (describes the material): Iron Vein: "Dark, heavy iron ore streaked through grey stone." / Ancient Oak: "Seasoned oak timber, hard as stone and older than the village."
- **Dungeon:** Duneglass Tomb (ancient): "A tomb of the ancient race, half swallowed by the dunes and still guarded." / Widow's Thicket (lair): "A tangle of webbed brambles where giant spiders nest and feed."
- **Merchant** (shows that appearance's personality): "A cheerful trader whose cart rattles with odd wares from every road." / "A hooded trader who never says where the goods came from."
- **Boss** (hints at the key item): Ulmar the Storm Roc: "A roc so vast its pinions blot out the sun over the valley." / Vharok the Troll General: "A troll warlord whose chest glows with a heart that burns as hot as a forge."

## Never
- Give the hero a name, personality, backstory, feelings or dialogue.
- Invent made-up words outside the approved list, names for the ancient race or Sky City, or Sky City features.
- Write intro or tutorial text before the core mechanics are final.
- Use like, post, follow, scroll, viral, content or algorithm in player-facing text, or "feed" as a noun ("feed" as a verb is fine: "spiders nest and feed") (info cards excepted).
- Break the satire limits in Tone.
- Explain game mechanics (skimming penalties, max HP loss, recovery) in lore, or give dungeon curses a gameplay effect.
- Give natural monsters a creator or master.
